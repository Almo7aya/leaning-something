The machine, running

Everything else on this site explains the emulator. This one runs it — frame after frame, continuously, with a cost model behind each one. Change anything on the right while it is going and the consequences show up immediately in the graph.

Heavy frames genuinely take longer to play out, so a stutter is something you watch the machine hesitate on. The numbers are a teaching model rather than a benchmark — they are tuned to reproduce the behaviour the topics describe at magnitudes you can see.

What you are looking at

The graph is stacked by where the time went. Three of those four bands are pathologies you can trigger deliberately.

Pipeline thrash — the red band

Every distinct render state needs a compiled Vulkan pipeline. Push distinct pipeline states above cache slots and each frame evicts what the next one needs, so it recompiles the same shaders forever.

This is why cache-key design matters: a key that includes state which cannot affect the output creates entries that are secretly identical, and thrashes for no reason. Covered in topic 17.

Upload cost — the amber band

Cost scales with granularity, not with how much actually changed. Drag granularity from 4 KB up to 1 MB at a fixed write rate and watch the band grow — the same writes now drag whole regions across the bus.

The opposite extreme is not free either: fine granularity means far more protection changes and faults. That trade is the subject of topic 10.

Serialised compute — the grey band

Turn async compute off and the compute queues stop overlapping the graphics queue; they wait behind every batch, so the penalty grows with the draw count. At 90 draws that is enough to turn a 60 fps frame into a 30 fps one.

Real hardware has one graphics queue and 56 compute queues for exactly this reason — see topic 14.

VSync, and why 17 ms is worse than 16

With vsync on, a frame that misses the refresh window does not arrive a millisecond late — it waits for the next one. A 17 ms frame presents at 33 ms.

That cliff is why a small regression can feel like the framerate halved. Watch the screen readout say missed vsync while the graph bar has barely moved.
